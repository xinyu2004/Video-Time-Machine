## What you see

A frame much larger than four-perf 35mm, close to 1.43:1, a little wider than 4:3.

## Where it sits in the chain

This is still film pulled by perforations, not a television raster. Graeme Ferguson, Roman Kroitor, and Robert Kerr built the system. The first film was Tiger Child at Expo 70 in Osaka.

The camera uses 65mm. The print uses 70mm. The extra edge is for the sound tracks. The strip runs sideways, fifteen perforations to a frame, which is why it is called 15/70. The picture is about 69.6 × 48.5 mm. 69.6 ÷ 48.5 is about 1.43.

The 35mm frame is still four perforations, running vertically. See <TLink slug="film" />. Projection is still 24. See <PLink id="twenty-four" />.

There are no samples on this film. The “about 16K” figure is an estimate of how much detail the frame can hold, not a grid stored in the frame. The sample grid of a digital projector is on <PLink id="imax-digital" />.

## How to tell

The print is 70mm, it runs sideways, and one frame shows fifteen perforations.

## Can it be removed

This is a frame size. It is not one frame shot wrong.

## What to do

Keep 15/70 apart from the 35mm slit off the 1889 roll. The perforations are the same kind of mechanism. The year and the direction of travel are not.

## Do not

Do not write a pixel resolution onto this film.
