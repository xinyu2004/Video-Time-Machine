## What you see

People in a silent picture hurry, then dawdle, or the whole reel runs faster than life.

## Where it sits in the chain

The camera was cranked by hand. A slow crank records few frames of one second of action. The projector steps at a fixed rate, so that stretch is squeezed. A fast crank does the opposite and stretches the action.

What is common now was shot around the mid-teens and is shown at 24, so the whole reel looks fast. When both camera and projector were cranked, the projectionist could follow the camera. Once the rate was locked, the unevenness stayed in the reel. See <PLink id="twenty-four" />.

This is not 3:2. 3:2 takes a reel already shot at 24 and lays it onto 60 fields. See <PLink id="film-to-60" />.

## How to tell

The hurry follows the gaps between exposures. The frames do not repeat 2, 3, 2, 3.

## Can it be removed

If you know how much real time each frame took, the projection gaps can be put back. A reel already cut at 24 and married to later sound does not go back as one piece.

## What to do

Decide that the gaps are from the crank before you put the shot timing back.
