## What you see

Motion hitches 2, 3, 2, 3. Some video frames take their two fields from different film frames, and a weave combs.

## Where on the chain

Sixty fields were already there. See <TLink slug="mains" />. 3:2 lays 24 frames onto those fields. The subcarrier did not invent it. After NTSC color pulled the field rate to 60000/1001, film is first pulled by the same 1000/1001 to 23.976 and then spread across 59.94 fields. See <TLink slug="ntsc" />.

Four frames occupy ten fields: A for two, B for three, C for two, D for three. Paired into video frames that is AA, BB, BC, CD, DD. The two fields of BC and of CD come from different film frames, so a weave combs. That is not PsF, and it is not a camera field. See <PLink id="psf-vs-adjacent" />.

## How to tell

In the figure, AA, BB, and DD are repeats. BC and CD are mixed frames. The bar in telecine-23 pauses on the repeat.

## Can it be removed

While the cadence is intact, the film frames can be recovered. See <PLink id="ivtc-vs-deinterlace" />. Interpolation only softens the hitch. See <PLink id="judder" />.

## What to do

Match this figure first, then decide whether to recover the film frames or leave the hitch.

## What not to do

Do not treat 3:2 as a product of the color subcarrier. Do not split BC and CD as if a camera had shot them.
