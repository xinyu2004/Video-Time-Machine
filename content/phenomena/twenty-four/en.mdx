## What you see

Once sound is on the reel, the hurry no longer follows the cameraman’s hand. The picture runs at 24 frames a second.

## Where it sits in the chain

The perforations and the 4:3 frame were fixed in the 1890s. See <TLink slug="film" />. The crank was still by hand. See <PLink id="hand-crank" />. An optical track is printed beside the picture. The projector pulls the strip, and the track has to pass the slit at one speed. The rate locked at 24 frames a second. That step is the late 1920s.

The track takes a strip beside the picture. The Academy aperture is about 22 × 16 mm, ratio 1.37:1. Widescreen is a mask or a squeeze on the same four-perf strip.

Into 50 fields, the reel is pulled to 25. See <PLink id="film-to-50" />. Into 60 fields it is 3:2. See <PLink id="film-to-60" />.

## How to tell

An optical track sits on the edge, and the projection is a steady 24, not the crank’s hurry and dawdle.

## Lab

This page has no instrument. The uneven crank is on its own page.

## Can it be removed

24 fps is the projection rate. It is not one frame shot wrong.

## What to do

Keep the crank’s unevenness apart from this 24. One is the gaps at the camera. The other is sound locking the rate.

## Do not

Do not say the 1890s four-perf frame was 24 a second from the start.
