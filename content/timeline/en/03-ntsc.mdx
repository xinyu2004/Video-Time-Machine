title: "NTSC color and 29.97"
summary: "The subcarrier is 455/2 times line rate, the 4.5 MHz sound carrier is 286 times line rate, and the frame rate becomes 30000/1001."
order: 3
---

Color adds a narrow chroma signal on top of luma. NTSC carries Y with I and Q. The subcarrier has to sit in the gaps of the luma spectrum, so it is placed at a half-integer multiple of line rate and the phase reverses each line.

Monochrome line rate, 15750 Hz, is multiplied by 1000/1001. 1001 = 7×11×13, so the color frame rate is 30×1000/1001 = 30000/1001 ≈ 29.970 and the field rate is 60000/1001 ≈ 59.940. Color line rate fh ≈ 15734.266 Hz.

fsc = (455/2) × fh. 455 = 5×7×13, so fsc = 315/88 MHz ≈ 3.579545 MHz. The sound intercarrier is an integer number of lines: 4.5 MHz = 286 × fh, and 286 = 2×11×13. The beat between chroma and sound falls into the line structure as well. Select 525 in <DividerChain /> and turn on the color clock to see the same integers.

The phase of the color subcarrier is the hue. A building adds a longer reflected path, and weather and temperature change the electrical length of the path, so the phase that arrives is shifted. The color burst stays at the reference angle. However far the picture’s subcarrier has turned relative to that burst, the hue turns by the same amount. The tint knob is a person adding the opposite angle. When the path is still changing, one setting does not keep up.

Dot crawl and rainbows in a composite signal are the carrier sitting on top of luma, not a later compression block. US studio black was often raised by 7.5 IRE. Japanese NTSC uses 0 IRE.

59.94 against an integer 60 Hz lamp leaves a beat near 0.12 Hz. If the camera is not locked, bars from a 60 Hz lamp roll around in a few seconds. See <PLink id="slow-roll-5994" />. Drop-frame timecode is the same 1001, used as bookkeeping.
